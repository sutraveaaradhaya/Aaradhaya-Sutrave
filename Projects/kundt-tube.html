Textile Weave Structures in Kundt's Tube

Experimental research investigating the acoustic behavior of textile weave structures using a Kundt's tube and custom-designed experimental components.

Overview

Designed, prototyped, and assembled a physical Kundt's tube test apparatus to investigate how 3D-printed textile weave structures influence acoustic behavior. Responsible for complete hardware design and experimental setup execution, translating acoustic impedance research into a detailed 3D CAD model. Engineered custom functional components—including mounting flanges, hardware fasteners, and a precision reducer to interface the acoustic sound source with the primary PVC pipe—utilizing physical measurements and iterative 3D printing prototyping. Constructed the final operational apparatus to interface with data acquisition scripts developed by graduate research collaborators, establishing a functional physical platform for material absorption testing.

Experimental Design

Components for the experimental apparatus were designed using CAD and prepared for acoustic testing.

CAD & Fabrication

SolidWorks was used to develop components that are too costly for the project. We 3D printed the flanges and bolts to save money. We also used sound absorption tape to make sure no sound is leaked.

Acoustic Testing

The 3D printed components were incorporated into the experimental setup for acoustic testing.
